Continuing from [Books I Read in 2022](https://s1999u2.github.io/books-i-read-in-2022/), I want to provide a brief overview of the books I read in 2023. Last year, I spent a significant amount of time commuting and traveling for business, affording me ample opportunity to dive into books. As an agonizingly slow reader, my go-to choice for the past two years has been audiobooks. I anticipate a less hectic 2024, so I'm not setting a goal to read as many books this year.

## Favorites
* [A Short History of Humanity](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/54814844)
* [An Immense World](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/60186447)

## English Books
### [The Bible Unearthed](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/169479)
**[Israel Finkelstein](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/98755), [Neil Asher Silberman](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/98756)**\
I discovered this book through the [UsefulCharts](https://www.youtube.com/@UsefulCharts) channel. Having been raised in a Christian environment, it was interesting to gain a well-researched and fact-based perspective on the biblical world and its stories. However, I must say that the book is quite detailed, and I found it challenging not to get overwhelmed by the plethora of facts and data. One of the very few books I didn't finish.

**My rating**\

### [Fire in the Valley](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/1427580)

### [Dealers of Lightning](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/1101290)

### [An Immense World](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/60186447)

### [The Secret History of Christmas](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/63107517)
**[Bill Bryson](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/7)**\
Christmas is a wonderful time of the year, before winter becomes truly uncomfortable, cold, wet, muddy, and dark. The book is quite short (which is a good thing). It's definitely a fun collection of amusing facts and trivia about Christmas, though it's also a bit random.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕


### [Chokepoint Capitalism](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/60098290)
**[Cory Doctorow](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/12581), [Rebecca Giblin](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/6176147)**\
One of my favorite books from last year centers around the concept of [*Enshittification*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enshittification).

>Here is how platforms die:
>First, they are good to their users;
>then they abuse their users to make things better for their business customers;
>finally, they abuse those business customers to claw back all the value for themselves.
>Then, they die.

Once you understand this mechanism, what was once a vague observation and feeling over time becomes clear and tangible — if there's a word for it, it suddenly becomes a thing. And we all know Enshittification from countless examples. Nonetheless, the book is worth reading beyond the central idea, as the case studies provide interesting insights. Highly recommended!

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐


### [Rainbow Six](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/5720)
**[Tom Clancy](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/3892)**\
I listened to the audiobook version while exploring various parts of Yokohama and Tokyo on foot. For this purpose, it was an ideal auditory companion, as it didn't require too much attention (not a technical book) and it's relatively long (Tokyo is *very* big). Although I found the book overall entertaining, I must admit that the story is not very profound, and it's rather soaked with flat stereotypes like vegan eco-terrorists.

As a child, I found the Rainbow Six video game series (and all other Tom Clancy games) incredibly cool, so I bought the book in English in my early youth. Understandably, I had neither the vocabulary nor the stamina to read the book at that time - so I was glad to have finally read/heard it after all these years. Maybe I should watch the movie adaptation of *The Hunt for Red October* next...

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕



### [The Perfect Thing](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/56827)
**[Steven Levy](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/32131)**\
While not the most factual book, *The Perfect Thing* is an ode to the aesthetic and conceptual uniqueness of the iPod, as well as the revolution it sparked in personal consumer technology. *Everyone* at my school wanted one, and every manufacturer tried to somehow copy it but never succeeded. Critics have dismissed Apple as overpriced designer tech with no meaningful functions since those days, but its relevance to the world, and particularly to a new Apple that would later culminate in the iPhone, is undeniable.
(And although I would have killed to have an iPod of my own, it wasn't until the iPod Touch in 2008 that I had saved enough money to afford one - what a device!)

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭕


### [A Brief History of Japan](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34083601)
**[Jonathan Clements](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/11044)**\
I listened to the book in preparation for [my conference](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/12327/123270D/Noise-robust-registration-of-microscopic-height-data-using-convolutional-neural/10.1117/12.2644620.short#_=_) in Japan. It's a very interesting story, and the audiobook is also very well narrated. Of course, I've since forgotten all the facts, but I'd still recommend it.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕


### [A Walk in the Woods](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/9791)
**[Bill Bryson](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/7)**\
The book provided an engaging and easy read while I meandered through the woods surrounding Tokyo. Although Bill Bryson writes in a relatively light manner and some incidents appear too perfect to be factual, it serves as a pleasant diversion. Moreover, it certainly evokes a desire for hiking.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭕


### [Notes from a Small Island](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/28)
**[Bill Bryson](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/7)**\
A brief journey back in time to the United Kingdom of 30 years ago. Entertaining, yet somewhat drier than the aforementioned book. Perhaps the performance of the German narrator bothered me too much (I did not have access to the English version).

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕


### [Expecting Better](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/16158576)
**[Emily Oster](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/6583172)**\
The weakest book I read last year. Similar to financial guides (see below) and topics like nutrition, the field surrounding *children* also suffers from a poor signal-to-noise ratio; it's difficult or nearly impossible to find meaningful literature among all the charlatans, life coaches, and the "I'll show you how it *really* works" crowd. Emily Oster's book was recommended to me by several friends as *the* fact-based book that offers sensible advice based on evidence.
Unfortunately, it didn't work for me at all. To avoid going into a rant, I'll just say that I cannot recommend this book, and anyone looking for something meaningful on this topic will, regrettably, have to look elsewhere.

**My rating**\
⭐⭕⭕⭕⭕


### [The Skeptic's Guide to American History](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/24501241)
**[Mark A. Stoler](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/230010)**\
Not a traditional book, I listened to it as part of *The Great Courses*. It challenges many widespread views on U.S. history, yet it remains logical and comprehensible throughout. Certainly, it shouldn't be the only source for understanding U.S. history, but it's interesting to see a different perspective.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### [Vor dem Gesetz sind nicht alle gleich](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/59754830)
**[Ronen Steinke](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/6820004)**\
>Dystopian fiction is when you take things that happen in real life to marginalized populations and apply them to people with privilege.
[H Book Club blog](https://twitter.com/Hugo_Book_Club/status/1428729860676067328)

If you want to gain a better understanding of how marginalized groups (in Germany) are disadvantaged by the law, then this book is an eye-opening, yet saddening read. The modern constitutional state is a cultural achievement. And although it is often taken for granted, it should not be. However, a system should also be judged by how it treats the socially/financially weak and those who have no one to advocate for them or who have no voice.

A more cynical take by W.E.B. DuBois comes to mind: *A system cannot fail those it was never meant to protect*.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
### [Eine Geschichte der Welt in 100 Mikroorganismen](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/58651555)
**[Florian Freistetter](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/5819643), [Helmut Jungwirth](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/17199438)**\
There are literally too many fascinating microorganisms to list them all, and this book makes a selection of 100 special curiosities. Even that is too many for me to remember the wonders of the microcosm in any detail. It's definitely a fascinating journey worth recommending. However, the book also feels somewhat randomly assembled, and the editing is not ideal in my opinion (jokes are repeated or explained multiple times, etc.).

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭕


### [Eingefroren am Nordpol](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/55932796)
**[Markus Rex](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/3304191)**\
While the subject is genuinely fascinating and the travelogue intriguing, this book veers a bit too much towards being a personal diary rather than offering a description of the Arctic, its flora and fauna.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕


### [Teuer!](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/94157528)
**[Maurice Höfgen](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/20746949)**\
Just like with medicine (see above), I'm also a complete novice when it comes to economics and finance. I discovered Maurice Höfgen through the podcast [Jung & Naiv](https://www.jungundnaiv.de) that I value highly. The book is certainly one that is very much tied to a specific time (in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic and during the war in Ukraine) and offers little value for future years.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭕


### [Schaden in der Oberleitung](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/49479685)
**[Arno Luik](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/4650979)**\
Ah, Deutsche Bahn. Deutsche Bahn is so unreliable and mismanaged that reality has surpassed any jokes on the subject. Not to mention their pricing or app issues. And like many, I've had problems on practically every journey, ranging from mild annoyances to rage-inducing follies.

I thought this book would describe the current state of affairs (dilapidated infrastructure, delays and cancellations, comparison with other countries, etc.), as well as the causes and background of the current situation. Instead, with each page, this book turned more into a "back in my day" rant by an old white man who rejects any change or modernization.

He's entitled to his opinion, but I don't recommend reading it.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕

### [Kaufen Oder Mieten?](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/23402234)
**[Gerd Kommer](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/1107923)**\
Whether Gerd Kommer provides meaningful insights or not, I (unfortunately) cannot judge. However, his books don't come across as typical get-rich-quick schemes. They seem substantial and grounded in a wealth of empirical data, with relatively clear conclusions (and they acknowledge uncertainty where the data is limited or poor).

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### [Souverän investieren mit Indexfonds und ETFs](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/31815623)
**[Gerd Kommer](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/1107923)**\
Even more so than with housing (see above), good advice is expensive when it comes to long-term financial planning, e.g., for retirement. Let's hope that this book is not more style than substance and that it serves as a helpful guide for sustainable investing.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### [Die kürzeste Geschichte Deutschlands](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34757960)
**[James Hawes](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/57491)**\
Although I live in Germany and have grown up and attended school here, we learned relatively little about "the big picture" of German history (or the peoples who lived in this area in the past) in history class. The focus was often on specific periods or other countries, such as the Nazi era, the French Revolution, and again the Nazi era (the crimes of World War II are still omnipresent nearly 80 years after the war ended). Towards the end of the book, as it approaches the present day and thus also the period I have experienced myself, I'm not sure if I can agree with the author, but I believe this is a general problem with history. The picture, or at least the narrative, often becomes clearer with some distance.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
