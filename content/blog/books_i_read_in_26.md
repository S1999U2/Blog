Just as with the "Things I Learned in…" series, 2026 has been a much more fruitful year than 2025. I've got more spare time (and energy) for reading. Podcasts still take up most of my available "in between" time, but I read quite a few books that I enjoyed (and some that I enjoyed less 😄).


## Favorites
### [The Mind of a Bee](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/59149209-the-mind-of-a-bee)
**[Lars Chittka](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/3976122.Lars_Chittka)**\
Wow. Fascinating to see how a seemingly simple thing such as a bee can be so intricate.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐


## English Books
### [Everything Is Tuberculosis](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/220341389)
**[John Green](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/1406384)**\
It's the first book by Green that I've read. So I didn't know what to expect. But after seeing it highly praised in my corner of the internet, I gave it a go. Sadly, I really didn't care much for this one and found it rather tedious.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕


### [The Russo-Ukrainian War](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/63326676)
**[Serhii Plokhy](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/8125679)**\
I know, I read this book quite a bit after its publication - always risky with books about fast-changing, topical issues. But this is exactly why I don't really like these kinds of books. At the time of writing, things are murky; we don't know how things will turn out. Still, the author has to find some kind of storyline and cohesive narrative, which can sour like fresh milk after publication.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕


### [Number Go Up](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/123276708)
**[Zeke Faux](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/22001142)**\
I was surprised that a book on crypto, a topic I try to avoid altogether, was quite enjoyable. As with The Russo-Ukrainian War, many things have changed since the publication. But in contrast I think it still holds up pretty well.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭕


### [The Light Eaters](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/196774338-the-light-eaters)
**[Zoë Schlanger](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/20574597)**\
After The Mind of a Bee, I had high hopes that this book would be similarly interesting, this time about plants. Sadly, I found Schlanger rather annoying. I think you can really tell that the former was written by a zoologist and ethologist, while the latter was written by a journalist.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕

### [Foellig nerdiges Wissen](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/112979324)
**[Jens Foell](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/26398727)**\
I see myself as a nerd at heart. This would put me squarely in the target audience. On the other hand, I have a solid background in engineering and CS with a general interest in STEM. This makes the book rather boring and too surface-level for me.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭕⭕

### [Die Sprengung](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/246604575)
**[Oliver Schröm](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/695444), [Ulrich Thiele](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/662837)**\
Since I got back into reading in [2022](https://s1999u2.github.io/books-i-read-in-2022/), books on investigative journalism have become a much smaller part of my media diet. Still, I like Schröm's books and heard him on a [podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oHLQ0i7pt0I) about Nord Stream 2. Some of the book is (still) speculative. There are competing views and narratives out there (which are addressed in the podcast). But I found Schröm's book quite enjoyable.

**My rating**\
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭕
